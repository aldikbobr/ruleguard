How it works and limitations
  • Pairs are matched by title similarity, with vetoes for mismatched numbers, years, months, parties, direction, tournament stage, outcomes and proper nouns.
  • 🤖 marks pairs reviewed by AI (Gemini, free tier): it extracts each market's settlement terms with verbatim quotes (a quote not found in the rules is discarded) and then classifies the pair; it says “not sure” rather than “equivalent” when in doubt. Its accuracy against the labeled pairs is shown in the random-sample section. Pairs without 🤖, 🔍 or 🎲 fall back to keyword rules, which are signals to check, not conclusions.
  • 🔍 marks pairs reviewed in depth against the rule texts, with a scenario in which they settle differently. These reviews were written by an AI assistant (Claude) and have not been re-checked by a person yet. They were picked as illustrative examples, so they are not a random sample. 🎲 marks pairs from the random sample.
  • Raw spread = 1 − (YES on one venue + NO on the other), using ask prices from the APIs, without fees, book depth or slippage. In thin books it changes within minutes, so “Arbitrage traps” are ranked by the gap between mid prices (halfway between buying YES and buying NO), and markets with a YES/NO gap wider than 15 pts are excluded. No spread is shown for Manifold (play money).
  • Kalshi's API exposes a summary of the rules (rules_primary); the full contract terms may add details.
  • In the local version (node scripts/serve.mjs) prices update live, and the ↻ button on each pair refetches both markets and re-compares them in this page only; the saved snapshot changes on the next full refresh. Auto-refresh runs while the page is open and visible.
  • Informational prototype. Not financial advice.
